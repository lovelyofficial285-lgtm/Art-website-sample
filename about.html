The Hands Behind the Art

We exist to keep India's living craft traditions alive — by taking them straight from the workshop floor to your walls.

Our story

Born in a village haat

Kala Haat began at a weekly village market in Bihar, where we watched a Madhubani painter sell a week of work for the price of a pizza. That didn't feel right then, and it still doesn't.

Today we work directly with artisan families in 40+ villages — handpicking every artwork, paying fair workshop rates upfront, and handling the packing and shipping so the makers can stay where they belong: at their looms, wheels and easels.

"When a home in Mumbai buys a Madhubani piece, a household in Madhubani village keeps painting."

Why it matters

Tradition, not trend

Machine prints chase trends. Our artisans carry 500-year-old lineages — taught by grandmothers, refined over lifetimes. When these skills stop paying, they don't just disappear from shops; they vanish from memory.

Every commission we place keeps a child in the workshop, a grandmother respected as a teacher, and a village proud of what it makes.
